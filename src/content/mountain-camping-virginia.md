# Mountain Camping in Virginia: Where to Pitch Near Cumberland Gap

![Tent at a wooded Cumberland Gap campground](https://media.babylovegrowth.ai/blog-images/organization-28977/1794122651743_Tent-at-a-wooded-Cumberland-Gap-campground.jpeg)

The best mountain camping in Virginia comes in three kinds: state park campgrounds, national forest campgrounds, and national park sites. In the state's far southwest corner, Cumberland Gap National Historical Park has a developed campground with 150 sites and five free-permit backcountry sites. Lee County, where we live and write, is the gateway. Below you'll find what each option offers, what it costs, when it's open and how to avoid the usual mistakes.

## TL;DR

- Virginia State Parks have camping at 29 parks with more than 1,800 campsites. Full-service campgrounds are generally open from the first Friday in March through the first Monday in December.
- Wilderness Road Campground at Cumberland Gap is the main drive-up base in our corner of the state. It has 150 sites and is open year-round, with some loops closed in winter. Every site must be reserved through Recreation.gov.
- Backcountry campers at Cumberland Gap need a free permit, picked up in person at the Visitor Center.
- Store all food properly, expect temperatures 5 to 10 degrees cooler at elevation, and plan for the 14-day stay limits.

## The kinds of mountain camping Virginia offers

Virginia's mountains run from the Blue Ridge in the east to the long Appalachian ridges in the southwest. Camping there falls into a few styles, and picking the right one matters more than picking the right dot on the map.

**State park camping.** [Virginia State Parks](https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/state-parks/camping) offer developed campsites with tables, grills and bathhouse access, and many have electric and water hookups. Primitive sites generally have fire grills, picnic tables, pit toilets and non-potable water. Some parks add camping cabins with bunk beds and no heating or air conditioning, plus yurts with no electricity, heat or on-site water. Cabins have a two-night minimum, and yurts require two nights when Friday or Saturday is included. Some parks also have equestrian sites with horse stalls.

**National forest camping.** The [George Washington and Jefferson National Forests](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r08/gwj/recreation/camping-cabins) offer campgrounds ranging from highly developed with electricity to primitive with minimal amenities. Stays are limited to 14 consecutive days at Forest Service campgrounds and 28 consecutive days at concessionaire-run ones.

**National park camping.** Cumberland Gap National Historical Park, which sits where Virginia, Kentucky and Tennessee meet, has both a developed campground and backcountry sites. That is where most of this guide is focused.

## Camping at Cumberland Gap National Historical Park

The park's front-country option is [Wilderness Road Campground](https://nps.gov/cuga/planyourvisit/wilderness-road-campground.htm), about 3 miles from the visitor center off Highway 58 in Virginia. It has 150 developed sites (Recreation.gov lists 151), and 43 of them have electric hookups at 20, 30 or 50 amps. Comfort stations have hot showers and potable water. There is a dump station for reservation holders and a seasonal camp store.

### Dates and reservations

The campground is open year-round, but loops A, E through G, and O close from the Monday after Thanksgiving until the third Friday in March. That matters if you're planning a late-fall or early-winter trip: you can still camp, but you'll be in a smaller part of the campground.

All sites must be reserved and paid for through [Recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/camping/campgrounds/258617) or by calling 1-877-444-6777. There is no showing up and picking a spot, so book before you drive.

### What it costs

The NPS lists these nightly fees on its campground page:

| Site type | Nightly fee | Senior or Access Pass holders |
|---|---|---|
| Site with electricity | $24 | $12 |
| Site without electricity | $18 | $9 |
| Group site | $39 | n/a |

Fees can change, so confirm the current rate on the NPS page or at checkout.

### Site limits and rules

According to the park's [compendium](https://www.nps.gov/cuga/learn/management/compendium.htm), each Wilderness Road campsite allows a maximum of 2 vehicles, 4 tents or 8 people. Groups of 12 to 25 people with up to 4 vehicles use designated group sites only. Camping is limited to 14 days within any rolling 30-day period, and camping outside designated front-country and backcountry areas is prohibited.

## Backcountry camping near the Gap

If you'd rather carry everything in, the park has [five designated backcountry sites](https://www.nps.gov/cuga/planyourvisit/backcountry-camping.htm):

| Site | Capacity |
|---|---|
| Gibson Gap | 12 |
| Hensley Camp | 20 |
| Chadwell Gap | 12 |
| Martins Fork | 25 |
| White Rocks | 12 |

Permits are free and required, and you get them in person at the Visitor Center. Plan your drive around the Visitor Center's hours, because you can't sort this out by email from the trailhead.

The park's trail network is large enough to build a multi-day trip around. The NPS describes [more than 85 miles of trails](https://www.recreation.gov/camping/campgrounds/258617), ranging from easy quarter-mile strolls to challenging 10-mile backcountry hikes. Its [hiking page](https://www.nps.gov/cuga/planyourvisit/take-a-hike.htm) lists the 19-mile Ridge Trail from Pinnacle to White Rocks, so a ridge-line overnight with a stop at White Rocks is a natural pairing.

## Food storage and bears

Bears are active in the backcountry here, and the park requires that food be stored in vehicles, in solid camping units, or [suspended 10 feet above the ground and 4 feet from trees](https://nps.gov/cuga/planyourvisit/wilderness-road-campground.htm). Treat that as a routine part of setting up camp, as you would pitching the tent. Do it before dinner is cooked, not after dark.

![Camper suspends food safely among the trees](https://media.babylovegrowth.ai/blog-images/organization-28977/1794122658097_Camper-suspends-food-safely-among-the-trees.jpeg)

The same logic applies at Virginia State Parks and in the national forests. Keep a clean camp, store coolers in the car, and never leave food in the tent.

## Weather and what to pack

Summers in this region are hot and humid, with highs reaching the mid to upper 90s. Winters are mild but can bring snow from January through March. Higher elevations run 5 to 10 degrees cooler than the valleys, according to the [NPS campground page](https://nps.gov/cuga/planyourvisit/wilderness-road-campground.htm). So a July night on a ridge can feel noticeably different from the same night in town.

A practical packing approach:

1. **Layers for every season.** Even in summer, bring a warm layer for ridge camping.
2. **A rain shell and a tarp.** Mountain weather turns quickly.
3. **Water capacity.** Primitive sites often have non-potable water, so carry or treat what you drink.
4. **A food-storage plan.** Hard-sided container, car or hang system, decided before you leave home.
5. **Printed confirmation.** Cell service is patchy in the mountains, so keep your reservation and permit details offline.

## Choosing a campground: what to check

Before you book anywhere in Virginia's mountains, run through this short list:

- **Hookups.** Do you need electric or water? At Wilderness Road only 43 of 150 sites have electric.
- **Bathhouse access.** Developed sites have it, primitive sites generally use pit toilets.
- **Site size.** Check whether the site fits your tent count or rig. Virginia State Parks allow six people per site, and Wilderness Road allows eight.
- **Reservable or first-come.** Wilderness Road is reservation-only. Other places mix both.
- **Season.** State park full-service campgrounds are generally open from the first Friday in March through the first Monday in December. The exceptions open year-round: Douthat, Hungry Mother, Pocahontas and Shenandoah River. Primitive campgrounds are open year-round.
- **Check-in and check-out.** Virginia State Parks use 4 p.m. check-in and 1 p.m. check-out.
- **Stay limit.** State parks cap camping at 14 days in a 30-day period.

## Beyond Cumberland Gap: other Virginia mountain options

If you're driving through the state, Shenandoah National Park lists five campgrounds, including Big Meadows with 221 sites, Loft Mountain with 207 and Mathews Arm with 165. They operate seasonally, and opening dates shift from year to year, so check the park page for the current schedule. Shenandoah campgrounds are generally reservable through Recreation.gov up to six months ahead.

In the southwest, the national forests offer a wide range of developed and primitive campgrounds, and the state parks cover the Blue Ridge and the Allegheny Highlands. ## Camping with horses in Lee County

Riders have a good option close to the Gap. [Rock Bottom Horse Camp](https://discoverleeva.com/directory/rock-bottom-horse-camp-ewing) in Ewing is listed in our directory, and it pairs well with a day of trail riding. The national park also supports horseback riding on its 17-mile Ridge Trail. If you're bringing animals, confirm the details with the camp directly before you load the trailer.

## When you'd rather sleep indoors

The NPS says that apart from camping, [there are no accommodations in the park](https://www.nps.gov/cuga/faqs.htm). Nearby towns such as Middlesboro, Kentucky, Cumberland Gap, Tennessee, and Tazewell, Tennessee have lodging. On the Virginia side, our [places to stay in Lee County](https://discoverleeva.com/lodging) page lists options, which is useful if the forecast turns or if part of your group prefers a bed. One of our listings is the [Wolfe-Gilbert House](https://discoverleeva.com/directory/wolfe-gilbert-house-dryden) in Dryden.

## What to do once the tent is up

Camping near the Gap rewards you with more than a place to sleep. The park offers [guided tours of Gap Cave and Hensley Settlement](https://www.recreation.gov/camping/campgrounds/258617), Pioneer and Civil War Living History demonstrations, and the panoramic Pinnacle Overlook. It also has picnic areas and reservable picnic shelters.

Shorter walks suit families. The [NPS hiking page](https://www.nps.gov/cuga/planyourvisit/take-a-hike.htm) lists the 0.7-mile Greenleaf Nature Trail loop at Wilderness Road Campground, the 2-mile Fitness Trail, and the 1.0-mile Lewis Hollow Trail to Skylight Cave.

Outside the park, Lee County adds ATV riding on the Stone Mountain Trail and a long list of overlooks. Our [outdoor things to do](https://discoverleeva.com/outdoors) page covers these. If your crew includes kids, our [family vacations in Virginia](https://discoverleeva.com/articles/family-vacations-in-virginia) guide helps with planning. History fans can go deeper with our [Lee County heritage](https://discoverleeva.com/heritage) page.

## A sample three-day plan

**Day 1.** Arrive at Wilderness Road Campground, set up camp, store food properly and walk the Greenleaf Nature Trail loop before dinner.

**Day 2.** Drive to the Pinnacle Overlook for the panoramic view, then take a ranger-led program if one is offered. Check the park's schedule, since tours and demonstrations vary by season.

**Day 3.** Hike one of the longer trails, or move to a backcountry site such as White Rocks with a free permit from the Visitor Center. On the way out, stop in Lee County for a meal. Our directory lists places such as [Patio Drive-In](https://discoverleeva.com/directory/patio-drive-in-pennington-gap) and [El Centenario](https://discoverleeva.com/directory/el-centenario-pennington-gap) in Pennington Gap.

## FAQ

### Can I camp inside Cumberland Gap National Historical Park?

Yes. Wilderness Road Campground has 150 developed sites and is open year-round, with some loops closed from the Monday after Thanksgiving until the third Friday in March. The park also has five backcountry sites that require a free permit from the Visitor Center.

### How do I reserve a site at Wilderness Road Campground?

All sites must be reserved and paid for through Recreation.gov or by calling 1-877-444-6777. Fees listed by the NPS are $24 per night with electricity and $18 without, so check the current rates when you book.

### When is Virginia mountain camping season?

Virginia State Parks' full-service campgrounds are generally open from the first Friday in March through the first Monday in December. Douthat, Hungry Mother, Pocahontas and Shenandoah River state parks are open year-round, and primitive campgrounds are open year-round. National park and forest campgrounds set their own seasons, so check each page.

### Do I need to worry about bears?

Bears are active in the backcountry near Cumberland Gap. Store food in a vehicle or a solid camping unit, or suspend it 10 feet above the ground and 4 feet from trees. Do this every time you leave camp and every night.

### How long can I stay?

Cumberland Gap limits camping to 14 days within any rolling 30-day period. Virginia State Parks use the same 14 days in 30. Forest Service campgrounds in the George Washington and Jefferson National Forests limit stays to 14 consecutive days.

# Virginia Motorcycle Trips: Best Rides, Roads and Planning Tips

![Motorcyclist riding a curve on Virginia’s VA-16](https://media.babylovegrowth.ai/blog-images/organization-28977/1794035112697_Motorcyclist-riding-a-curve-on-Virginia-s-VA-16.jpeg)

The best Virginia motorcycle trips fall into three groups: the long mountain parkways in the Blue Ridge (Skyline Drive and the Blue Ridge Parkway), the tight technical roads in the southwest (the Back of the Dragon and the Claw of the Dragon loop), and the gentle history rides in the east (the Colonial Parkway and the SR-5 and SR-10 route). Pick by the kind of day you want. This guide covers each road with its length, rules and fees, then shows how to stitch them into a multi-day trip that ends at the far western tip of the state, in Lee County.

## TL;DR

- **Mountain views, easy pace:** Skyline Drive (105 miles, 35 mph in most places) and the Blue Ridge Parkway (469 miles, no entrance fee).
- **Twisties:** the Back of the Dragon on VA-16 is 32 miles and 438 curves, and the Claw of the Dragon loop around Wytheville adds more than 350 miles of curves.
- **History:** the 23-mile Colonial Parkway links Jamestown, Williamsburg and Yorktown.
- **Before you go:** Virginia requires an approved helmet for riders and passengers, Shenandoah charges a $25 motorcycle pass, and fuel stops are limited on the parkways.
- **Finish west:** Route 58 runs to Cumberland Gap, where Lee County offers lodging, food and quiet back roads.

## Blue Ridge rides: Skyline Drive and the Blue Ridge Parkway

These two roads are the classic Virginia ride, and they connect. [Skyline Drive](https://www.nps.gov/shen/planyourvisit/driving-skyline-drive.htm) runs 105 miles along the crest of Shenandoah National Park and takes about three hours end to end in clear conditions. It has four entrances: Front Royal, Thornton Gap, Swift Run Gap and Rockfish Gap. The Blue Ridge Parkway picks up where Skyline Drive ends. It is a [469-mile National Parkway](https://www.blueridgeparkway.org/) from Milepost 0 at Skyline Drive to Milepost 469 at Newfound Gap Road in Great Smoky Mountains National Park.

The [Blue Ridge Parkway motorcycle itinerary](https://www.blueridgeparkway.org/itineraries/motorcycle-tour-skyline-drive-blue-ridge-parkway/) counts 75 overlooks on Skyline Drive and names Waynesboro and Rockfish Gap as the northern Virginia gateway to the Parkway. Its Virginia stops include Humpback Rocks Visitor Center, the James River Visitor Center and Otter Lake, Thunder Ridge Wilderness, and Peaks of Otter Lodge.

If you want a ready-made two-road weekend, Lonely Planet describes a [225-mile Front Royal to Roanoke route](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/best-road-trips-in-virginia) on Skyline Drive and the Parkway. It suggests Staunton, Lexington and Bedford as overnight bases.

![Blue Ridge rides: Skyline Drive and the Blue Ridge Parkway](https://media.babylovegrowth.ai/blog-images/organization-28977/1794035157674_Blue-Ridge-rides-Skyline-Drive-and-the-Blue-Ridge-Parkway.jpeg)

### What to expect on both roads

These are slow roads by design. Skyline Drive's limit is 35 mph in most places, and the National Park Service warns that deer, black bears and wild turkeys cross the road. If you want to look at an animal, pull completely off the pavement. The Blue Ridge Parkway tops out at 45 mph, according to [a planning guide](https://blueridgemountainstravelguide.com/plan-a-blue-ridge-parkway-road-trip/), which also notes that gas stations sit off the Parkway, so top up in a valley town before you climb.

Inclement weather can close Skyline Drive at any time. The Park Service says you can [text SHENALERTS to 888777](https://www.nps.gov/shen/planyourvisit/driving-skyline-drive.htm/index.htm) for alerts, and it notes that filling-up options on the Drive are limited. Marys Rock Tunnel at mile 32.2 has a 12'8" clearance, which matters only if you ride with tall luggage or a trailer.

## Southwest Virginia: the Back of the Dragon and the Claw

When riders search for Virginia motorcycle roads, this is usually what they mean. [Visit Smyth County](https://visitsmythcountyva.com/must-sees/small-towns-with-big-time-charm/) describes Marion as a gateway to the Back of the Dragon, a 32-mile route that crosses three mountain ranges with switchbacks, slaloms and hairpins. [Adventure Rider](https://www.advrider.com/virginias-back-of-the-dragon/) puts VA-16 at 438 curves and 3,500 feet of elevation change. It also notes that the road mixes technical corners with open sweepers and the occasional straight, so it is not one uniform type of bend. That variety is why experienced riders keep coming back.

If you want more than one road, the [Claw of the Dragon loop](https://visitwytheville.com/claw-of-the-dragon/) is centered on Wytheville and runs through Wythe and Smyth counties toward Galax. It offers more than 350 miles of curves, farmland, forested climbs, river views and historic downtowns. The [Blue Ridge Parkway's Claw itinerary](https://www.blueridgeparkway.org/itineraries/claw-of-the-dragon-road-trip/) adds stops worth a break from the handlebars: the 16-mile scenic ascent of Big Walker Mountain, the 100-foot Big Walker Lookout tower, Wytheville's historic district, Beagle Ridge Herb Farm and West Wind Winery.

### Another technical option: the Alleghany Highlands

Rider Magazine's [Alleghany Highlands account](https://ridermagazine.com/alleghany-highlands-virginia-motorcycle-ride/) recommends a one-day loop from downtown Clifton Forge on SR-606. The writer rode the tight ridge switchbacks in first and second gear, on a clear, dry spring day. Stops include the Historic Masonic Theatre, Falling Spring Falls (an 80-foot drop you can see from a roadside pull-off), Humpback Bridge, Lake Moomaw and the C&O Railway Heritage Center, in a late-1800s freight depot.

## Eastern Virginia: history on two wheels

The Colonial Parkway is a gentler ride. The [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/colo/parkway.htm) describes it as 23 miles from the York River at Yorktown to the James River at Jamestown, finished in 1957, with a 45 mph maximum. Its tunnel, built in 1942, has a clearance of 10'4" in the Jamestown-bound direction. Lonely Planet describes the route as running through [undeveloped coastal woodland, tidal channels and marshes](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/best-road-trips-in-virginia), with Jamestown, Colonial Williamsburg and Yorktown Battlefield as natural stops.

For a longer day, [RoadRunner Travel](https://www.roadrunner.travel/tours/nov-dec-25-classic-roads-nickel-and-dime/) describes a 125-mile SR-5 and SR-10 tour between Richmond and Williamsburg. It uses tree-lined SR-5, rural SR-10 and the free, 20-minute Jamestown-Scotland Ferry, passing plantation houses along the way.

## Finish in the far west: Route 58 to Lee County

Most lists stop at the Blue Ridge, but Virginia's long east-west road keeps going. A [Law Tigers rider guide](https://lawtigers.com/the-motorcycle-rides-virginia-riders-love-the-most/) describes Route 58 through the Southern Highlands as a ride of five hours or longer from Meadows of Dan heading west. It passes mountain towns such as Galax and Damascus and reaches Virginia's western border at Cumberland Gap National Historical Park, where you can climb to Pinnacle Overlook.

That border is Lee County, and it makes a natural end to a Southwest Virginia trip. Ride the Back of the Dragon one day, then spend the next day or two heading west on Route 58 and settle in at the end. Our [Appalachian mountain road trip guide](https://discoverleeva.com/articles/appalachian-mountain-road-trip) lays out the Cumberland Gap side of that journey, and our [Lee County guide](https://discoverleeva.com/guide-to-lee-county-virginia) covers the area in full.

### A sample five-day plan

1. **Day 1:** Ride Skyline Drive from Front Royal to Rockfish Gap, about three hours without stops. Overnight in Staunton or Waynesboro.
2. **Day 2:** Take the Blue Ridge Parkway south toward Roanoke, with stops at Humpback Rocks and Peaks of Otter.
3. **Day 3:** Head to Wytheville or Marion and ride the Back of the Dragon.
4. **Day 4:** Follow Route 58 west through Galax and Damascus toward Lee County.
5. **Day 5:** Rest day or short ride in Lee County, with a walk to Pinnacle Overlook.

The days are deliberately short. Mountain roads tire you faster than the mileage suggests, and you will want time to stop.

## Rules, fees and safety before you ride

### Fees and access

The Blue Ridge Parkway needs no entrance pass. According to the [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/blri/planyourvisit/fees.htm), a charge applies only for camping in its campgrounds. Shenandoah is different: the [fees page](https://www.nps.gov/shen/planyourvisit/fees.htm) lists a $25 motorcycle pass valid for seven days, and the park does not accept cash. Bring a card.

### Helmets, gear and licensing

The Virginia DMV's [motorcycle manual](https://transactions-t.dmv.virginia.gov/drivers/motorcyclemanual.asp) says operators and passengers must wear approved helmets. Operators also need a face shield, safety glasses or goggles, or an approved windshield. The same manual recommends sturdy ankle-covering boots, gloves and eye protection, plus rain and cold-weather gear, because cold or wet conditions cause numbness and fatigue that hurt your control. If you hold a learner's permit, the manual says it is valid for one year and allows riding only between 4 a.m. and midnight, with supervision by a qualified licensed motorcyclist, subject to stated family exceptions.

### Speed

Virginia's [safe driving guidance](https://dmv.ar.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/documents/dmv39d%20.pdf) sets a 55 mph maximum for motorcycles on roads with no other posted limit. It also says reckless driving can include driving 20 mph over the limit or above 85 mph. On curvy mountain roads the posted limits and your own sight distance are the real constraints.

### Weather and timing

Check road status before you leave, especially for Skyline Drive, which can close in bad weather. Morning cold in the mountains can be sharp even on a sunny day, so carry a layer you can add. Since cold and wet conditions reduce control, plan to start later and end earlier on cool days.

## Packing and planning checklist

- **Fuel:** fill up before Skyline Drive and the Parkway. Gas is limited on the Drive and off the Parkway.
- **Payment:** carry a card for the Shenandoah pass, since cash is not accepted.
- **Gear:** approved helmet, eye protection, gloves, ankle-covering boots and a rain layer.
- **Wildlife:** expect deer and bears near the road, and pull fully off to look.
- **Clearance:** note the tunnel heights on Skyline Drive and the Colonial Parkway if you carry tall luggage.
- **Lodging:** book ahead for weekends in popular gateway towns.
- **Alerts:** sign up for Shenandoah text alerts the day before you ride.

## FAQ

### What is the best motorcycle ride in Virginia?

It depends on what you like. Skyline Drive and the Blue Ridge Parkway are best for views, the Back of the Dragon is best for curves, and the Colonial Parkway is best for history. Many riders combine the first two in a single trip.

### How long does Skyline Drive take on a motorcycle?

The National Park Service says about three hours end to end in clear conditions. Plan longer if you want to stop at overlooks, since the road has 75 of them. Check conditions first, because weather can close the road.

### Do motorcycles have to pay to ride the Blue Ridge Parkway?

No entrance fee applies to the Parkway, and charges apply only for camping. Shenandoah National Park is different, with a $25 motorcycle pass valid for seven days.

### Does Virginia require a helmet?

Yes. The Virginia DMV says operators and passengers must wear approved helmets. Operators also need eye protection, such as a face shield, goggles, safety glasses or an approved windshield.

### Is there good riding near Cumberland Gap?

Route 58 runs west through Southwest Virginia mountain towns to Cumberland Gap National Historical Park. Lee County is the natural place to stay and explore at the end of that ride.
